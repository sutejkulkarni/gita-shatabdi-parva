Gita Shatabdi Parva

Gita Shatabdi Parva celebrates a century of Kalyan, the revered spiritual and cultural magazine of Gita Press. First published in 1926, Kalyan has, for a hundred years, carried the timeless wisdom of Sanatana Dharma, reaching generations through its writings on devotion, knowledge, dharma and righteous living. As the magazine completes its centenary in 2026, we honour this remarkable journey and the enduring legacy of Gita Press. This century of service also stands alongside Gita Press’s recognition with the Gandhi Peace Prize for 2021, acknowledging its contribution to peace, social harmony and the preservation of India’s spiritual heritage.

The Bhagavad Gita

The Bhagavad Gita, the timeless dialogue between Sri Krishna and Arjuna, has found its way into countless homes through Gita Press. With over 16 crore copies published, it remains the Press’s most widely circulated work. For generations, the Gita Press edition has been a familiar presence in Indian households, carrying the eternal message of dharma, wisdom and devotion from one generation to the next.

Krishna & Arjuna

When Bhishma’s attack overwhelmed the Pandava army, Krishna rose in fury, lifting a broken chariot wheel despite His vow not to wield a weapon. As He charged toward Bhishma, Arjuna held Him back, reminding Krishna of His sacred vow. Bhishma, filled with devotion, welcomed the sight of the Lord rushing toward him.

Sheshashayi Vishnu

Upon the boundless cosmic ocean, where all creation lies in silence, Lord Vishnu rests upon the thousand-hooded serpent Shesha, the eternal protector of the universe. From His divine navel rises a magnificent lotus, blossoming above the primordial waters. Seated upon it appears Lord Brahma, destined to bring forth the worlds, beings and all of creation. Thus, from the serene and eternal presence of Narayana, the cycle of creation begins—born from the divine, sustained by the divine, and destined to return to the divine.

Lord Ganesha

When Sage Vyasa resolved to narrate the vast and timeless Mahabharata, he sought a scribe capable of recording his words without pause. Lord Ganesha agreed, but placed a condition: Vyasa must narrate continuously, without stopping. Vyasa accepted, with a condition of his own—that Ganesha must understand every verse completely before writing it. And so began a divine feat of endurance and wisdom: as Vyasa poured forth the epic, Ganesha's pen raced across the pages, preserving the story of kings, warriors, dharma and destiny for all generations to come.

Krishna and the Cows

In the sacred pastures of Vrindavan, Sri Krishna spent His days among the cows, lovingly tending them and enchanting them with His flute. The Vedas hold the cow in deep reverence, describing her as a symbol of nourishment, abundance and sacredness. Thus, Krishna’s bond with the cows reflects the timeless Vedic ideal of compassion, protection and harmony with all creation.

Go Raksha, Rashtra Raksha

In our tradition, the cow is revered as a symbol of nourishment, compassion and selfless giving. From the days of Krishna in Vrindavan, caring for and protecting cows has been seen as an act of seva. Today, this spirit can live on through small acts of kindness—offering food, supporting a goshala, or helping a cow in need. Every contribution, however small, can become meaningful when offered with sincerity. If you have the opportunity to support the protection and care of cows, do so wherever you can. In serving them, we honour a timeless tradition of compassion and gratitude.
